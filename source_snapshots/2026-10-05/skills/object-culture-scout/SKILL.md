# Object & Culture Scout

## When to use
Before Adaptation Blitz Match, when choosing what a carousel might be about. This skill finds objects and the worlds they live in. It does not write the carousel, synthesize a premise, or pick finalists.

## Job
Find what people currently want, recognize, or argue about. An object can be a product, a configuration, a routine, or a combination. It does not have to be a new SKU, and it does not have to be in the news. Current means currently sold, or still part of ordinary life.

## Order
Breadth before depth. One easy category must not fill the pool just because its pages expose better metadata. Prefer many promising worlds with some unresolved fields over a few perfectly documented objects from one category.

1. WORLD BREADTH. Look across materially different worlds before spending real effort on any one object. Worlds might include beauty, clothing, home, bedroom, grocery, food, pets, kids, family, electronics, gaming, school or work, household, fitness, collectibles, hobbies, gifting, or seasonal. That is a reminder to diversify, not a checklist.
2. OBJECT. Inside promising worlds, find several current objects. A page that fails to show one field does not kill the object. Try a proportionate alternate path, then keep it and mark the unresolved field. Do not spend the breadth budget recovering one exact number.
3. BEHAVIOR AND CULTURE. As soon as a world or object looks promising, search how people use it and what goes wrong: what they misunderstand, argue about, regret, return, substitute, gift, share, hide, or compare, and what social expectation surrounds it. Search colloquially, before exact verification is finished. This is behavioral evidence, not a finished-story database. If one community page will not open, that is not proof the behavior is absent. Use another review, forum, social, search, editorial, or comment source.
4. TRANSACTION AFFORDANCES. Note the purchase artifact the behavior would already produce, such as an order, receipt, return, pickup, delivery, quantity, variant, gift receipt, substitution, cancellation, price, payment, or confirmation. If none, say none.
5. HARDENING. Exact facts wait until a later concept survives and actually depends on them.

## Price ladder
Never invent a number. Do not turn an approximate price into an exact retailer price. Exact cents are not required here. A useful ingredient must not be discarded only because an exact current price cannot be extracted. If the reaction itself depends on the price, say so, and harden it before Gate 1 or mark that dependency. Otherwise the price can wait.

1. EXACT CURRENT RETAILER PRICE. Direct current evidence from the retailer.
2. CURRENT INDEXED RETAILER PRICE. Current indexed or search evidence clearly attributable to that retailer.
3. CURRENT APPROXIMATE PRICE OR RANGE. A current reliable listing establishes the economic stake. Label it approximate.
4. PRICE UNKNOWN. Verify only if a concept survives.

## Composable evidence
One source does not have to prove the whole ingredient. Keep provenance on each fact. Do not imply that source A supports fact B.

- Object or availability comes from the retailer, or from indexed retailer evidence.
- Price comes from the ladder above.
- Desire or cultural heat comes from community, social, search, review, or editorial evidence.
- Category behavior comes from reviews, forums, social discussion, comments, or other behavioral evidence. It may be about the category. Nobody has to have discussed the exact listing.
- Fulfillment behavior comes from the retailer's own documentation.

These may sit together when the relationship is ordinary and legitimate. Object verified, retailer presence verified, price unknown, and behavior promising is a valid ingredient.

## Currentness
Do not attach stale behavioral evidence only because nothing newer would open. Label it when useful: current signal, persistent behavior, or stale and weak. A durable ordinary behavior may stay usable when the source is older. A temporary trend needs current evidence.

## Two kinds of heat
NOVELTY HEAT. New, limited, scarce, seasonal, or just released. This needs a current signal.
BEHAVIORAL HEAT. People are repeatedly ordering it, showing it, copying it, or arguing about it. A persistent pattern is enough.
Label each find NOVELTY, BEHAVIORAL, or BOTH. A famous brand alone is not enough.

## Do not censor the search
Surface in-store-only objects, objects with no commercial path, and pure cultural opportunities. Do not drop an object because it cannot carry an offer. Commercial classification happens later, in Adaptation Blitz.

## Bind search, alongside the object
Also look from a real constraint or desire toward the object and its actual access or price, when evidence shows people are already in that situation. Do not wrap a hot product in a made-up bind. An unverified price or deadline is not a price. Leave it unknown.

## Anti-anchoring
Do not prefer an object because a file already exists. Ask what you would want if the library were empty.

## Output, not a carousel
For each opportunity: OBJECT, WORLD, CULTURAL HEAT TYPE, WHY NOW, DESIRE, BEHAVIOR OR CULTURE, TRANSACTION AFFORDANCE or none, PRICE STAKE as exact, indexed, approximate, or unknown, CURRENTNESS, SOURCE PROVENANCE BY FACT, UNRESOLVED FIELDS, COMMERCIAL OPPORTUNITY or none, CONFIDENCE.

COMMERCIAL OPPORTUNITY names a natural surface or says there isn't one. It does not pick the affiliate winner.

## Facts later
This relaxes early completeness only. It does not authorize fabrication. Unknown stays unknown until verified. Before production, any factual detail actually shown or relied upon gets the appropriate verification. If a surviving concept depends on a fact that later fails, revise or reject the concept.

Then stop. Hand the list to Adaptation Blitz, including objects that cannot carry an offer.
